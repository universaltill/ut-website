One till is enough until it isn't: a queue at lunchtime, a second counter, a
terrace, a tablet for the kitchen. Universal Till lets you add more tills to
the same shop without a network engineer and without a cloud account. One till
is the **main till**; the others **join** it and share the catalogue, prices,
settings and stock automatically, over your shop's own network.

## Pairing a new till in a minute

1. On the main till, go to Settings → Tills and press **Show pairing code**. It
   shows a short code (like **K7P-4XQ**), the main till's address underneath,
   and a QR code. The code works once and expires after 10 minutes.
2. Install Universal Till on the new device, choose **Join an existing shop**
   and give it a name, such as "Bar" or "Terrace".
3. Either type the code and the address, or press **Find a primary on this
   network** and pick your main till from the list. If you search, both
   screens show the same 6-digit number: check they match, and a manager
   approves the request on the main till. Pairing is never automatic, so a
   device on your Wi-Fi can't quietly join your shop.

The new till then copies the whole shop across (products, prices, settings,
stock and staff), which can take up to a minute on a busy network. It is given
its own register automatically, so staff can open a shift on it straight away.

## What the tills share

- **Sales** from every till flow back to the main till, so the whole shop's
  sales end up in one place.
- **Catalogue changes** can be made by admins and managers on any till, not just
  the main one. They're saved on the main till and reach the others within
  seconds (half a minute at most). If two people edit the same item, the second one is asked to reload
  instead of overwriting the first person's change.
- **Shop-wide settings** (payment options, the receipt design, the lock timeout
  and so on) are kept on the main till too. Settings that belong to one till,
  like its printer or its screen, stay on that till.
- **Stock** is counted by the main till. An additional till never refuses a sale
  because its own copy of the stock number is a few seconds behind. If two tills
  sell the last one at the same moment, it shows up on the main till's dashboard
  instead of blocking a customer.
- **Gift vouchers** work shop-wide: sell one on any till and spend it on any
  other till that can reach the main till, and two tills can't spend the same balance at once.
- **Plugins** follow the main till: install one there and every joined till
  downloads and checks it within about half a minute.
- **Updates** follow the main till as well. An additional till moves to the
  main till's version at the next moment it has no open sale, or shows a note
  where it can't install updates by itself yet (on Android, for example).

## When a till loses touch

On an additional till, the status bar has a light that always says in words
what it means: **Main till linked**, or **Main till not reachable since … —
selling offline**. Either way, that till **keeps selling**. Its sales wait on
the till and sync as soon as the main till is back. While it's out of touch,
the shop-wide settings show read-only, with a note saying why, so nobody makes a
change that would be lost.

If the main till comes back on a different network address after a restart,
the additional till finds it again on its own. It only reconnects to the main
till it was paired with, so another device can't take its place. If your Wi-Fi
stops devices from seeing each other, an additional till can also ask your
shop's cloud account where the main till last said it was, as long as both
tills are connected to the cloud.

## Order boards, kitchen displays and self-order

Extra devices don't have to be tills in the usual sense. A tablet can be a
**self-order** screen where customers build their own order, or an **Order
status** board showing orders from every till as they come in. A station's
**kitchen display** runs on the till that takes its orders, on a second screen
plugged into it. An order board or kitchen display keeps showing and updating orders through service, and asks for a PIN
only if someone tries to open anything else.
