A new till often starts with a hardware quote: a terminal, a stand, a contract.
Universal Till doesn't need any of that. It's software you install on a device
you choose, and the core is **free, with no monthly fee**. Its core is open
source under the MIT licence, so nobody can take it away from you either.

Here's what it runs on today, and what to know about each option.

## Windows, Mac and Linux computers

**Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit).** Run the guided installer and Universal Till opens
in its own app window from the Start menu or a desktop shortcut: no terminal, no
browser tab. A new installation opens **full screen** with the taskbar hidden,
and starts when you sign in to Windows, so a spare PC behind the counter behaves
like a dedicated till. A manager PIN (or Alt+F4) always gets you back to the
Windows desktop, and you can change both choices under Settings → Display.

**Mac with Apple Silicon (M1 and newer).** Download, open, and the till runs in
its own window. The first time, macOS may block an app downloaded outside the App
Store: open System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose **Open Anyway**.

**Linux.** Install the `.deb` and "Universal Till" appears in your applications
menu, in its own window. There's also a download for ARM boards other than the
Raspberry Pi. That spare laptop in the back office can become a till.

## Raspberry Pi: a small, quiet till

A **Raspberry Pi 4 or 5** running 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS makes a compact till
with no fan noise. Install the `.deb` and:

- On **Raspberry Pi OS Lite** (no desktop), a fresh install sets up a
  **fullscreen kiosk** automatically on first boot. The Pi starts straight into
  the till.
- On **Raspberry Pi OS with Desktop**, the till opens fullscreen on top of the
  desktop at login, and Settings → Display → **Exit to OS window** (with a
  manager PIN) takes you back to the desktop.

One thing to get right: the power supply. A Raspberry Pi 5 needs the official
27 W USB-C supply. An older, weaker charger fits the socket but can make a
touchscreen miss taps or freeze. The till checks for this and shows **"Power
supply too weak"** on the status bar, so you're not left guessing.

## Android tablets and phones

The Android app runs on **Android 7.0 and newer**. You install it from our
download page rather than the Play Store, so Android will ask you to allow
"unknown sources" the first time. That's normal for an app installed outside
the store.

A tablet makes a good counter till, a self-order kiosk your customers can use,
or an **Order status** board showing orders from every till as they come in.
On a phone, the sale screen shows your items as large
icons with a **Pay** bar at the bottom, so you can take a sale from your pocket
at a market stall or a busy table. When an update is ready, the till hands it
to Android's own installer and you confirm it there.

iPhone and iPad apps are in testing and aren't generally available yet.

## Scanners, printers and the camera you already have

- **Barcode scanners:** a USB scanner works as soon as it's plugged in. A
  **Bluetooth** scanner or scale pairs from inside the till: scan, tap
  **Pair**, done.
- **No scanner at all?** On any device with a camera, tap the camera-scan icon
  next to the scan box and point it at the barcode. The picture never leaves
  the device.
- **Receipt printers:** pick **Network (IP)** for a Wi-Fi or Ethernet thermal
  printer (the till can search your network for it), **USB device** for one
  plugged in, or **Regular printer** to print plain-text receipts on the
  computer's own default printer.

Whatever you run it on, the till works [without an internet
connection](/en-gb/blog/offline-pos-keeps-selling-without-internet/). On
most platforms it also updates itself overnight unless you switch that off, and
it never restarts in the middle of a sale.

guesses the right file for you. Or browse the [plugins](/en-gb/plugins/) that
add what your shop needs.
