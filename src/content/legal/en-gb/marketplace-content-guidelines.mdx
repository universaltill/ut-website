These guidelines say what a listing in the Universal Till marketplace (a plugin or other item a shop can install on its tills) may and may not do. They are part of the [marketplace publisher terms](/en-gb/legal/marketplace-terms/). Task Runner Technology Ltd ("we", "us") runs the marketplace; see our [legal notice](/en-gb/legal/impressum/).

## What a listing must not do

- **Malware or harm.** No malicious code, no code that damages a till, its data or a shop's network, no hidden remote control, no attempt to get around the checks that sign and verify plugins.
- **Deception.** No false or misleading name, description, screenshots, version, publisher or claims. A listing must do what it says and must say what it needs.
- **Illegal content.** No content or function that is unlawful where it is offered, and nothing that helps someone break the law.
- **Harvesting personal data.** No collecting, sending or selling of personal data about shops, staff or customers beyond what the listing's description says and the shop agreed to. No hidden tracking.
- **Bypassing payments or age rules.** No taking payment outside the payment methods the till offers in a way that dodges app store rules, and no way around age checks or other restrictions a shop has switched on.
- **Abuse of other people's rights.** No copying of another publisher's work, brand or identity without permission.

## Artificial intelligence

A listing may use artificial intelligence only in line with how the product treats it: the shop chooses its own provider and supplies its own key, a self-hosted option is the default, and no feature may depend on AI or on a Universal Till account. A listing must say plainly if it sends data to an AI provider, and must not send data to one the shop did not choose.

## How to report a listing

Every listing page has a **Report this listing** link. Choose a reason, describe what is wrong, and add a contact address if you want a reply. You do not need an account.

## What we do with a report

We read it, may ask for more information, and decide. We may ask the publisher to fix the listing, hide it, remove a release, withdraw the listing, stop installed copies from running, or close the publisher's account. We tell the publisher what we did and why where we can, and the publisher may appeal as the [publisher terms](/en-gb/legal/marketplace-terms/) set out.
